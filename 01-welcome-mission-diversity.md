# Section 1: Callaghan Brain and Body Lab Manual

## 1.1 Welcome

**Welcome to the Brain and Body Lab at UNSW Sydney!**

We are so glad that you are joining us, in whatever capacity it is (undergraduate, research assistant, HDR candidate, postdoc, visiting scholar, or collaborator). Academia can be a confusing place, and no two labs are the same. We have written this manual to make your transition into the BABLab as smooth as possible, to share the goals of the lab, its inner workings, as well as its unique culture, to which you will contribute. We hope that during your time here, you will learn a lot about the brain and its interactions with the rest of the body, about developmental psychology, and about science in general. It is our collective goal to have everyone leave the lab with more than they started with, whether that's new skills, a fresh perspective on a given topic, or a network of colleagues and friends. We hope that you make it your goal to place your own positive stamp on the lab, and to contribute to it in a meaningful way. Just like us, a lab grows and develops over time, and each individual shapes its path. We look forward to seeing what your time in the lab will bring.

This manual was originally written at UCLA, and has been revised for the lab's new home at UNSW Sydney in 2026. It was inspired by several others when first created, and borrows heavily from them (e.g., this one by the Aly Lab and this one from the Ritchey Lab were the primary sources and the main templates used, along with a few others).

A lot of time has gone into this document — thinking about what we want our lab to be, what we want to be known for, and how we can make the lab work for everyone. We really respect the principles and ideas we're aspiring to here, and for that reason, we ask that all new lab members read the manual in full.

## 1.2 Mission Statement

In the BABLab we aim to do good science that makes a difference. That is our mission. To do it, we need people to be passionate about what we are doing and come to work in a good frame of mind to get it done. We aim to be nurturing, challenging, and stimulating to students. We aim to be respectful, engaging, and meaningful for families and participants.

## 1.3 Diversity Commitment

We in the BABLab value the diversity of our lab members and research participants. We are committed to learning, understanding, and dismantling systems of oppression, particularly in academia, and to being genuine allies to First Nations peoples and other historically marginalised communities. You are welcome in this lab whatever the colour of your skin, your sexual orientation, your gender identity, your nationality, your ability, and your culture.

We acknowledge the Bedegal (Bidjigal) people as the Traditional Custodians of the land on which UNSW's Kensington campus stands, and pay our respects to Elders past and present. Sovereignty over this land was never ceded.

**Supporting Indigenous participation in research and academia.** We are committed to engaging with UNSW's [Nura Gili Centre for Indigenous Programs](https://www.unsw.edu.au/nura-gili) to identify pathways for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander students to take part in the lab's research (e.g., through Indigenous Honours or HDR pathways coordinated with Nura Gili), and to consulting Nura Gili early whenever a study's design, recruitment, or data involves Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander participants or communities. Any such research will be conducted in line with the [AIATSIS Code of Ethics for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Research](https://aiatsis.gov.au/research/ethical-research) and the NHMRC's [Ethical Guidelines for Research with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/research-policy/ethics/ethical-guidelines-research-aboriginal-and-torres-strait-islander-peoples).
