Section 5: Data Access and Management

Where lab data lives

Day-to-day project files (protocols, in-progress analysis, wiki drafts, non-final data) live on UNSW OneDrive/SharePoint (Microsoft 365), which is approved for research data from Public up to Highly Sensitive classifications — provided multi-factor authentication is enabled, which it should be by default on your UNSW account. Everything stored there stays on servers physically located in Australia and is retained for a minimum of seven years, with a 60-day recovery window if something is accidentally deleted. Classify data honestly when you create it:

  • Public — things like published papers, de-identified aggregate summaries.
  • Private — most day-to-day working files.
  • Sensitive — identifiable participant data, unpublished results.
  • Highly Sensitive — anything where a breach could cause real harm (e.g., identifiable data on minors, health information, anything a participant would reasonably expect strong protection for).

Sensitive and Highly Sensitive data may need additional encryption or access restrictions beyond the OneDrive/SharePoint default — check with RDM@unsw.edu.au (UNSW’s research data management team) if you’re not sure what’s required for your project, and ask before you start collecting, not after.

Access permissions

Not everyone needs access to everything. As a rule: raw, identifiable data is restricted to people named on the relevant ethics approval for that project (see Section 3); de-identified/cleaned datasets can be shared more widely within the lab once they exist. When the lab manager sets up a new project’s folder structure, permissions should be set at that point, not left open by default. If you’re leaving the lab or rolling off a project, your access should be reviewed and removed if it’s no longer needed.

Long-term archiving and sharing

For data the lab is finished actively working with:

  • Smaller behavioural/questionnaire datasets: the Open Science Framework remains a good option, alongside UNSW’s own Research Data Archive for long-term institutional preservation.
  • Neuroimaging data: OpenNeuro.
  • Microbiome data: the Sequence Read Archive or European Nucleotide Archive.
  • Studies with specific funder data-sharing mandates (e.g., NIH-funded studies inherited from UCLA, or any future NHMRC/ARC-funded work) will have their own requirements — check with Bridget before sharing anything from those studies specifically.

As before: share code and data freely within the lab (aligning with ethics requirements), but don’t share anything with the outside world until the lab has finished working with it and Bridget has agreed it’s ready.

Retention

Under the Australian Code for the Responsible Conduct of Research, research data and primary materials must generally be retained for a minimum of 5 years from publication (longer for clinical or otherwise higher-risk research — check the specific requirement for your project type). In practice, UNSW’s default 7-year retention on OneDrive/SharePoint covers this, but retention obligations survive even if someone leaves the lab, so departing lab members cannot take the only copy of anything with them.

Moving data across institutions and borders

If you’re bringing a dataset with you from a previous institution (e.g., UCLA), or collaborating with researchers overseas, that’s a genuine cross-border data transfer, not just a file copy. It needs a data-sharing/data-transfer agreement between the institutions (talk to Bridget and UNSW Research Services before moving anything with identifiable participant information), and it needs to be covered by the relevant HREAP approval at UNSW as well as whatever approval covered the original collection.

Devices and everyday security

  • Use UNSW-managed or UNSW-approved devices and accounts for anything involving participant data — not personal laptops, personal cloud storage, or personal email.
  • Use a password manager and unique passwords; enable MFA everywhere it’s offered.
  • Never leave identifiable data (paper or digital) unattended in testing rooms or common areas.
  • If you lose a device, or suspect any account or data has been compromised, tell Bridget and the lab manager immediately — this may need to be reported to UNSW IT and, depending on what’s involved, to the relevant HREAP.

Approved tools

Data collection tools (e.g., Qualtrics, REDCap) and any new software should be checked against UNSW’s approved-vendor and data-storage-location requirements before adoption. Ask the lab manager or RDM@unsw.edu.au before locking in a new tool for a study.
